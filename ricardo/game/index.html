What each piece really means (say this in your breakout room)
England & PortugalRicardo's two countries. Portugal has the absolute advantage in both goods — it's simply more productive. The lesson is that this does not mean it should make everything.
Looms vs. vineyardsYour opportunity cost. England gives up little wine to make cloth (bad at wine anyway), so cloth is England's comparative advantage. Full specialization in cloth is the counter-intuitive winning move.
Welfare meterYou need both goods, so welfare rewards a balanced basket. Making 40 cloth and zero wine is useless — until you trade half of it for wine.
"Alone" baselineAutarky — self-sufficiency. The most England can reach making everything itself is ~14.1. Any welfare above that line is a pure gain from trade.
Terms of tradeThe price in the deal (wine per cloth). It must sit between the two countries' home costs (½ and 1½). Inside that band, both nations gain; outside it, one side refuses. This is the harmony Ricardo proved.
Portugal's refusalPortugal signs only if the deal beats its own autarky (36.7). So every deal that gets signed makes both countries richer — the gains from trade are real, not taken from anyone.
Where the gains goPush the ratio toward 1½ and you capture most of the gain; toward ½ and Portugal does. The split of the gains is a negotiation — a small echo of Ricardo's bigger theme that distribution is where the conflict lives.
A learning model of Ricardo's comparative advantage (Principles, 1817, ch. 7). Productivity numbers are simplified for play, not Ricardo's exact labor-hours. The point is real: specialize by opportunity cost, trade inside the band, and both sides win.  ·  Explainer · All builds
